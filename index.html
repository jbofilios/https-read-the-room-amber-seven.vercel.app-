      Avoid

      Three rules

      Down before right

      Never move someone diagonally. A sharp question to someone who’s heated lands as cross-examination, and they dig in. Lower the temperature first: acknowledge their position, name what’s at stake for them, and slow your own pace. Once they settle into the Fortress, invite them across with curiosity about their reasoning.

      Shared inquiry, not cross-examination

      Socrates used questions to expose contradictions, and Athens eventually put him on trial for it. If a question has a gotcha answer you’re steering toward, people feel it. Only ask what you’re genuinely curious about, and swap “why did you…” for “what led you to…”.

      Map yourself first

      Energy is contagious. If you walk in as an Explorer and the other person is asserting, your enthusiasm lifts them straight into Defender. Check your own quadrant before the meeting and lower your pace on purpose.
